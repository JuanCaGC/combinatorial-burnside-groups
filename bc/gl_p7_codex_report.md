# Certified p=7 top-piece computation

All work was run in `/Users/juancagc/Groups/task-gl-p7`. No git commands were run. The target is the top piece `B.bn_quotient([p,p],2,Vector{Int}[])`, not the full combinatorial Burnside group.

## Results

For p=7 the abelian group is

$$M=(\mathbb Z/14)^3\times\mathbb Z^{159}
=(\mathbb Z/2)^3\times(\mathbb Z/7)^3\times\mathbb Z^{159}.$$

The torsion subgroup is invariant. The extension of the free quotient by the torsion subgroup does not split equivariantly. Its reductions to both primary torsion components are nonsplit.

The 7-primary torsion module is

$$\mathbf 1\oplus\det^2\oplus\det^4\quad\text{over }\mathbb F_7.$$

It is reducible, with three one-dimensional composition factors. It has image of order 3 and is neither the natural module nor its dual, already ruled out by its dimension 3 versus 2. This is not a single scalar determinant character on a three-dimensional space. It is a direct sum of three different determinant characters.

The 2-primary torsion module also factors through the order-3 determinant quotient. It has a trivial factor of dimension 1 and an irreducible factor of dimension 2 over GF(2). Since its image has odd order 3, this module is semisimple. Both primary actions are the three-dimensional cyclic permutation representation of that quotient.

The free quotient, after tensoring with the complex numbers, has 18 distinct ordinary irreducible constituents and 21 constituents counted with multiplicity:

- Three distinct cuspidal constituents of degree 6, each with multiplicity 1.
- Three distinct twisted-Steinberg constituents of degree 7, each with multiplicity 1.
- Twelve distinct principal-series constituents of degree 8. Three have multiplicity 2 and nine have multiplicity 1.
- No linear constituents.

The dimension check is $3(6)+3(7)+(3(2)+9)(8)=159$. The character norm is $3+3+3(4)+9=27$, equal to the computed inner product. This is an ordinary character classification, not a decomposition of the integral lattice into irreducible lattices.

## Certification and action convention

The supplied scripts and the complete GL-module section of REPORT.md were read before creating files. `gl_action_p7.jl` was copied and adapted from `gl_action_p5.jl`. The primitive root is computed by enumerating multiplicative orders, yielding 3. Before any quotient computation, GAP verified that the generators T, D=diag(3,1), and S generate a group of order 2016. The subgroup generated by T and D has order 42.

GAP matrices are formed from integer entries multiplied by `Z(p)^0`. No occurrence of `Z(7)^2` is used to encode the integer 2.

The certified action checks the following using exact integer matrices:

- Full Smith certificate `U_full*A*V_full == D_full`, with both transforms unimodular.
- Residual Smith certificate `U*M*V == D`, with both transforms unimodular.
- `J*E == I`, original relations mapping into the residual relation lattice, residual relations mapping into the original relation lattice, and the difference from the original lift-project identity lying in that lattice.
- Final quotient maps satisfying `L*Q == I`.
- Equality with the abelian group returned by a separate untracked quotient call.
- Preservation of the relation lattice by all three generators, and agreement of the induced action with the quotient maps.
- All 2016 elements times all three generators, totaling 6048 exact composition checks, with torsion columns compared modulo their invariant factors.

The permutation action is evaluated as `L*Q[tuple_permutation,:]`. This is exactly `L*P*Q` for the original permutation matrix P and avoids repeatedly allocating dense permutation matrices. Every element is computed directly, and cached for the product checks.

The first certified run took 23.752 seconds inside Julia and 37.228 seconds including startup. After fixing the serialization handoff described below, the second run took 22.837 seconds inside Julia and 36.184 seconds including startup. The second run stage timings were presentation 0.006595 seconds, elimination 0.148094 seconds, full Smith form 0.465957 seconds, residual Smith form and quotient certificates 0.149756 seconds, and generator/action-product work 14.977210 seconds. Compilation and output account for remaining time. Both runs passed all certificates.

Classification enumerates the actual GAP matrix group and its right-multiplication edges. It builds both BFS assignments using the character permutations, then compares their induced quotient matrices against the action computed directly from each of the 2016 group matrices. Thus this is a comparison of the actual quotient matrices, not merely generator relations or permutation equality.


The consistent convention is $R_{gh}=R_hR_g$. The genuine homomorphism used for module identification is $g\mapsto R_g^T$. The character traces are unchanged. The report does not rely on BFS contradiction counts alone.

## Torsion blocks, determinant action, and splitting

Coordinates are obtained from the computed invariant factors `[14,14,14,0,...,0]`. No free rank of 46 or p=5 dimension is assumed.

For every generator, the torsion-row/free-column block is zero. This proves that the torsion subgroup is invariant and that the free block defines a quotient action. For every generator, the free-row/torsion-column block is nonzero modulo 14. This says that the selected free lifts are not invariant. A further computation is needed to decide whether other lifts could split the extension.

Write the row matrix in block form as $R=\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\C&F\end{pmatrix}$. A section of the free quotient has row matrix `[X,I]`, and it is invariant exactly when $XA-FX=-C$. The code solves the consistency problem for all three generators together, separately over GF(2) and GF(7). There are 1431 equations in 477 unknowns in each case. In both cases the coefficient rank is 477 and the augmented rank is 478. No section exists. This proves nonsplitting, independently of the choice of free lifts.

The full torsion group is not a GF(7)-vector space. Its characteristic primary parts are analyzed separately, with reduction of the order-14 coordinate matrices modulo 2 and modulo 7. MeatAxe is called using `GModuleByMats` over each field and the transposed generator matrices.

Exhaustive comparison over all 2016 elements gives zero violations of dependence only on determinant, for both primary parts. Let C be the genuine matrix for D, printed in the raw MTX output. It is a 3-cycle matrix. Since 3 generates the nonzero elements of GF(7), the action for determinant $3^k$ is $C^k$. Equivalently, determinants 1 and 6 act by I, determinants 3 and 4 act by C, and determinants 2 and 5 act by $C^2$. The raw determinant table prints row matrices, which are the transposes of these genuine matrices. Eigenvalue multiplicities for D over GF(7) verify the determinant powers 0, 2, and 4, each once.

## Ordinary character computation

GAP constructs `CharacterTable` directly from the same matrix group. Character values are the traces of the directly computed free blocks at representatives in the table's own class order. Inner products are computed in GAP with complex conjugation. Each multiplicity is converted with GAP `Int` before conversion to Julia, and nonnegativity is checked. No cyclotomic numbers or `fail` values are passed to `gap_to_julia`.

The computed degree counts are 6 linear, 6 twisted-Steinberg of degree 7, 15 principal series of degree 8, and 21 cuspidal of degree 6. They agree with $q-1$, $q-1$, $\binom{q-1}{2}$, and $q(q-1)/2$ at q=7. Their degree squares sum to $6+6(49)+15(64)+21(36)=2016$. Dimension 159 and character norm 27 are asserted in the program.

In this run, the constituent triples `[table index, degree, multiplicity]` are printed in the raw output. These indices identify the actual irreducibles in the computed table and are not canonical labels across independent GAP sessions. Specific inducing-character labels for the principal and cuspidal series have not been assigned. The family identification uses the distinct degrees at q=7.

## Failure, limits, and scope

The first classification attempt failed before performing classification. Julia's standard serialization invoked OSCAR's matrix IPC serializer, and deserialization in a new process failed with a missing process-local UUID. The complete traceback is preserved below and in `gl_classify_p7_attempt1_output.txt`. The fix exports matrices as ordinary Julia `Matrix{BigInt}` arrays and reconstructs OSCAR integer matrices in the receiving process. The certified action was rerun with all checks intact. The second classification run passed in 11.361 seconds inside Julia and 23.795 seconds including startup.

No command timed out. The quotient run had a 180-second limit. Each certified action run and classification run had a 900-second limit. All Julia runs use `gl_run_r3.py`, `julia --project=.`, and `gl_bootstrap.jl`, with scratch, temporary files, package usage logs, and history redirected into this isolated copy. The wrapper emits a mongoc allocator warning, preserved in the raw outputs. It did not cause a nonzero exit in successful runs.

The p=3 and p=5 comparison uses the supplied existing results and does not revalidate their extension claims. In particular, nonzero mixed blocks alone are insufficient to prove the p=5 nonsplitting assertion in the older prose. That caveat does not affect the independently checked nonsplitting result for p=7.
